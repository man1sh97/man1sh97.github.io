I work on stochastic dynamics, active matter, and nonequilibrium statistical physics, with a particular interest in the dynamics and thermodynamics of active particles.

Research

A central theme of my research is to understand the physical mechanisms underlying transport, fluctuations, and dynamical transitions in systems far from equilibrium. I am particularly interested in the interplay of activity, inertia, confinement, interactions, and competing dynamical timescales, and in how these ingredients give rise to phenomena that have no direct equilibrium analogue. I combine analytical theory, stochastic dynamics, and physically motivated modeling to develop quantitative descriptions of such complex nonequilibrium behavior. My work has focused on developing analytical approaches to characterize non-Gaussian fluctuations, re-entrant phenomena, anomalous and non-monotonic diffusion, and crossovers between distinct dynamical regimes. I am also interested in how interactions and non-reciprocal couplings modify the dynamics and transport properties of active systems. These questions provide a natural connection to biological physics, where persistent motion, fluctuations, non-reciprocal interactions, and nonequilibrium driving are intrinsic features of living systems.
