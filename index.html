Research

A central theme of my research is to understand how activity, inertia, interactions, confinement, and competing dynamical timescales shape the behavior of nonequilibrium systems. I am particularly interested in phenomena that have no direct equilibrium analogue and in developing analytical and computational approaches to describe them quantitatively.

01 · Active Matter

Dynamics and transport of active Brownian particles, active chains, and interacting active systems, with emphasis on activity-induced transitions and anomalous transport.

02 · Stochastic Dynamics

Exact and analytical approaches to stochastic nonequilibrium dynamics, including higher-order moments, non-Gaussian fluctuations, and dynamical crossovers.

03 · Inertia & Resetting

Understanding how inertia and stochastic resetting control transport, localization, and steady-state properties of active particles.

04 · Nonequilibrium Transport

Diffusion, non-reciprocal interactions, and competing active and thermal driving in systems far from equilibrium, with connections to biological and soft-matter systems.

My work spans single-particle dynamics to interacting many-body systems, with an emphasis on identifying the physical mechanisms responsible for emergent transport and fluctuations.

I am also interested in extending these ideas toward biological physics, particularly cell and tissue dynamics, where persistent motion, fluctuations, non-reciprocal interactions, and nonequilibrium driving are intrinsic features of living systems.
