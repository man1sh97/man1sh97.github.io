I work on stochastic dynamics, active matter, and nonequilibrium statistical physics, with a particular interest in the dynamics and thermodynamics of active particles.

Research

A central theme of my research is to understand the physical mechanisms underlying transport, fluctuations, and dynamical transitions in systems far from equilibrium. I am particularly interested in the interplay of activity, inertia, confinement, interactions, and competing dynamical timescales, and in how these ingredients give rise to phenomena that have no direct equilibrium analogue. I combine analytical theory, stochastic dynamics, and physically motivated modeling to develop quantitative descriptions of such complex nonequilibrium behavior. My work has focused on developing analytical approaches to characterize non-Gaussian fluctuations, re-entrant phenomena, anomalous and non-monotonic diffusion, and crossovers between distinct dynamical regimes. I am also interested in how interactions and non-reciprocal couplings modify the dynamics and transport properties of active systems. These questions provide a natural connection to biological physics, where persistent motion, fluctuations, non-reciprocal interactions, and nonequilibrium driving are intrinsic features of living systems. My experience with analytically challenging stochastic systems and multiple competing timescales motivates me to explore increasingly complex nonequilibrium phenomena, particularly those arising in living matter. I am interested in understanding how microscopic fluctuations, interactions, and active processes become coupled across multiple spatial and temporal scales, and how such coupling gives rise to collective behavior and emergent dynamics. I view the inherent complexity of biological systems as an exciting theoretical challenge, where rigorous statistical-physics approaches can help identify the mechanisms underlying collective dynamics and function. My broader research goal is to develop a quantitative understanding of complex nonequilibrium systems, combining analytical rigor with physically informed modeling and, where possible, connections to experimentally accessible observables. I aim to build on my background in active-matter dynamics and nonequilibrium statistical physics to address challenging problems in active matter, soft matter, and biological physics, particularly those involving coupled processes across scales, strong fluctuations, and intrinsically nonequilibrium dynamics
